  • Native, not printed Tracks, audio, MIDI, fades, automation, stretch, pitch, reverse, warp markers and Melodyne edits come across natively, in every direction. Nothing is printed to audio.
  • Built-in effects Each DAW's built-in effects become the others' closest built-in effects. EQ, volume, pan and width match; compressors, limiters, reverbs and the like are close, not identical.

  • First visit ~50 MB The first visit downloads about 50 MB (Python and ffmpeg for the browser); after that they are cached. Large projects need a computer with enough memory (roughly three times the project's size).

  What converts supported · not yet
  

    

      
Supported

      
    
        
  • Projects: Cubase (.cpr), REAPER (.rpp) and Ableton Live (.als), in every direction, with every media file collected
    • 
        
  • Tracks: audio, instrument and MIDI tracks, folders and groups, names, colours, mute, solo, track delay
    • 
        
  • Clip colours: audio and MIDI clip colours in every direction
    • 
        
  • Mix: volume (past +6 dB too), pan with each DAW's pan law matched, sends and returns, output routing into groups (Live's Audio To becomes a Cubase group)
    • 
        
  • Audio clips: position, offset, clip gain, fades with their curves, reverse, playback rate, pitch, stretch and warp markers, overlapping clips
    • 
        
  • Comping: REAPER lanes and takes, Cubase track versions and Live take lanes carry across - every take kept, the comp playing (Live: take lanes with the comp on the main lane)
    • 
        
  • MIDI: notes, velocities, controllers; several instruments layered on one REAPER track (Cubase: a track per instrument under a group; Live: an Instrument Rack on the one track)
    • 
        
  • Automation: volume, pan and plug-in parameters
    • 
        
  • Arrangement: tempo, time signature, markers, regions (Live: locators)
    • 
        
  • Built-in effects: each DAW's own effects become the closest built-in effects of the others, settings carried - EQs, compressors, limiters, gates, expanders, de-essers, multiband dynamics, delays, reverbs, chorus, flanger, phaser, tremolo, auto-pan, pitch shift, distortion, saturation, bit reduction, stereo width, gain. Levels are matched by measurement
    • 
        
  • Third-party plug-ins: VST3 and VST2 with their settings; CLAP plug-ins as their VST3 build
    • 
        
  • VST2 ⇄ VST3: a plug-in can be written as its other build, settings carried (FabFilter, Native Instruments, iZotope, Serum, soothe2, KORG and more - 72 products so far)
    • 
        
  • Melodyne: edits come across natively
    • 
      

    

    

      
Not yet / not possible

      
    
        
  • Cubase effects with no equivalent elsewhere: Vocoder, VocalChain, Pitch Correct, FX Modulator, AutoFilter, DualFilter, ToneBooster, Grungelizer, Metalizer, RingModulator, StepFilter, Tranceformer - left out with a note; render the track in Cubase first
    • 
        
  • Live devices with no equivalent: Corpus, Resonators, Vocoder, Beat Repeat, Looper, Spectral Resonator, Spectral Time, Erosion, Max for Live devices
    • 
        
  • VST2 in Cubase: Cubase 15 loads only VST3, so a VST2 always arrives as its VST3 build
    • 
        
  • VST2 ⇄ VST3 not yet for: Omnisphere, Stylus RMX, Arturia CS-80 and plug-ins not in the list - they stay in the format they were saved in
    • 
        
  • Plug-ins must be installed on the machine that opens the converted project. Converting to Live on a computer with Live installed picks the build Live can load there (VST2 or VST3); in the browser, choose the build yourself if a plug-in does not open
    • 
        
  • Not sample-identical: pitch-shifted or stretched clips and the "close" effects use each DAW's own algorithms - they sound alike and match in level, but do not cancel exactly. Synths with random elements never repeat exactly, even in their own DAW
    • 
        
  • Live limits: one clip at a time per track (overlaps go on a copy track), fader up to +6 dB (the rest goes on a Utility)
    • 
        
  • Fade curves in Live are all S-shaped, so a REAPER or Cubase curve becomes the nearest Live curve (and comes back as it was)
    • 
        
  • REAPER project offset (a timeline starting before bar 1) is not carried to Cubase - synths that follow the song position (tempo-synced LFOs, arps) can sound slightly different
    • 
        
  • Colours are matched to the nearest one where a DAW has a fixed palette (Cubase's project colours, Live's 70 clip colours)
    •
